Anatomy of a 1,000-call campaign

What happens between "upload a contact list" and "every customer has been reached" — which component does what, why the system keeps its promises when parts of it fail, and where the backups sit. Written for engineering teams evaluating the platform.

1 · The journey of 1,000 calls

A campaign is not 1,000 API calls fired in a loop. It is one durable plan the platform executes on your behalf — paced against real capacity, inside your calling window, with per-contact accounting from upload to final report.

STEP 1

Upload

1,000 contacts arrive — CSV, API, or CRM push. Each row is validated (phone format, required fields), de-duplicated, and stored. Nothing dials yet.

STEP 2

Pre-flight

Before the first ring: is the account funded for the run? Is AI capacity online? Is the calling window valid? Any failure stops the campaign here — at zero cost.

STEP 3

Queue

All 1,000 contacts sit in a time-ordered queue. A contact at rest consumes no compute — 1,000 or 100,000 waiting rows cost the same: nothing.

STEP 4

Pace

The orchestrator claims exactly as many contacts as there are free lines — your concurrency setting, capped by live AI capacity. Never more.

STEP 5

Dial

Each claimed contact becomes one durable call task: reserve capacity → dial via the telephony gateway → connect the voice engine the moment the customer answers.

STEP 6

Converse

Speech recognition, the language model, and speech synthesis run the conversation in real time — all inside India. The control plane never touches audio.

STEP 7

Record & retry

Every outcome is written per contact. No-answer and busy re-enter the queue at now + retry interval, up to your retry limit. Connected calls never re-dial.

STEP 8

Report

Live counters while running; signed event webhooks to your systems per call; a full per-contact report and export when the campaign completes.

Timing you can plan around. 1,000 contacts · 5 concurrent lines · ~40% pickup · ~90 s connected / ~30 s ringing ≈ 3 hours of calling, before retries. Wall-clock scales with lines, not list size: 20 lines ≈ 45 minutes.
Calling windows are enforced by the platform, not by your scheduler. Outside the window the campaign sleeps on a durable timer and wakes at the next slot — a 7 pm cutoff holds even through our own deployments.

3 · The pacing engine

Why 1,000 contacts cost nothing at rest: 995 of them are inert rows in a time-ordered queue. Only the handful inside your concurrency limit exist as live work. Backpressure is the queue itself; a retry is one re-queue with a future timestamp.

Window closes mid-run → the campaign sleeps on a durable timer. Deployments, restarts, even a full control-plane reboot do not cancel it — it wakes at the next slot and continues from the exact contact it stopped at.
A claim is not a deletion. A claimed contact whose call never happens returns to the queue when its lease expires. A crash can delay a contact by minutes; it can never lose one, and it can never dial one twice.
Done means accounted. Queue empty + zero live calls → campaign completes, a completion event fires, and the per-contact report reconciles: every row ends in exactly one terminal state.

4 · Why it is durable

The design rule behind everything: a dial attempt is a durable task, not a loop iteration. Processes are disposable; state is not.

Journaled execution

Every step of every call is appended to a replicated journal (Temporal on PostgreSQL) before it runs. If an orchestrator dies mid-campaign, a replacement replays the journal and resumes from the exact step — mid-campaign, mid-call-attempt.

Leased claims

Taking a contact off the queue is an atomic claim with a lease, not a pop. Two workers can never take the same contact; a claim that is never completed returns to the queue on lease expiry. Nothing is lost between "claimed" and "dialed".

Idempotent effects

Each call has one identity end to end. A re-delivered dial step cannot place a second call (the workflow engine rejects the duplicate), and a re-delivered billing step cannot charge twice (debits are keyed on the call ID).

Durable timers

Calling windows, retry intervals, and maximum call durations are journaled timers in the workflow engine — not in-process timers. A 7 pm cutoff survives anything short of losing the journal itself.

Degrade to "not placed"

If AI capacity is unavailable, the call is simply not placed and the contact waits. The system never places a call it cannot staff — your customer never answers the phone to silence.

One exit gate

Every call — connected, missed, failed, or aborted — terminates through a single code path that writes the final record, applies at most one debit, and emits one event. The books cannot drift from reality.

7 · The guarantees, in one line each

No contact lost — crash-safe queue with leases
No contact double-dialed — atomic claims + one call identity
Exactly-once billing — idempotent debits per call
Calling windows enforced platform-side
Do-not-call checked before every dial
Never dead air — a call is not placed unless it can be staffed
All audio stays in India
Every event delivered or dead-lettered — never dropped
The one-sentence summary for your architects: the campaign is a journaled plan executed by disposable workers against leased capacity — so any single component can die at any moment, and the worst case is a delay, never a lost contact, a double dial, or a double charge.
